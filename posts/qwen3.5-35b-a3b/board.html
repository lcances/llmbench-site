llmbench · qwen3.5-35b-a3b · budget tier · rev 2 — bench-box runs 2026-08-02/03

20 GiB of weights on a 16 GB card, decoding like the 9B that fits. The trick is not how much you offload — it is what.

Sixth and last rung of the Qwen3.5 ladder, and the only mixture of experts in the family: 35B total parameters, ~3B active per token, 256 experts spread over 40 blocks. It decodes a few tokens per second behind the 9B at every depth — which is the remarkable result, not the disappointing one, because this model does not fit the card and the 9B does. It is also the only model in this series whose first dataset was thrown away: offloading whole layers — the knob every dense model in the ladder used — evicts the attention blocks that run for every single token along with the experts, of which eight of 256 fire. Moving the knob to which expert tensors leave the GPU roughly doubled decode from the same GGUF file (measured 2026-07-30 on this dataset's previous revision — see the decisions log). Everything else in this post is downstream of that one decision, including the one genuine win ollama keeps in this series.

READ BEFORE QUOTING — this tree holds only the n_cpu_moe revision. The whole-layer-offload attempt (measured 2026-07-30, decode roughly half of what the MoE axis reaches — see the decisions log) is no longer in the tree, so this board cites it as history and never draws it. ollama cannot disable EOS or its prefix cache, so its decode comes from continuation chaining with an unload/reload per rep; that overhead sits inside its wall time and its energy figure. Probe-matched odd prompt sizes are reps=1 (no spread band). The max-context probes all ran at the single pinned n_cpu_moe = 15: a larger pin would free VRAM for KV cache, so these ceilings are the ceilings of this pin, not of the model. That limit is exactly what fig 05 is about. Finally, the reference:ollama-defaults row was re-measured on 2026-07-30: the comparator had been passing --threads 20 on the belief that ollama defaults to physical cores, when ollama passes no threads flag at all and its runner reports 4. On the four dense rungs that made no difference — nothing is on the CPU at full offload — but here it cost the comparator 40 % of its decode, so every earlier draft of this board reported a wrapper tax with the wrong sign.

Which tensors leave the GPU

The question the first attempt at this model got wrong. That attempt used whole-layer offload — bisect -ngl for the most layers that fit, the knob every dense rung in this series uses — which evicts attention, which every token needs, to make room for experts, of which eight of 256 fire. Moving the knob to expert placement (n_cpu_moe, all 40 attention blocks kept on the card) roughly doubled decode from the same GGUF file, measured 2026-07-30 on this dataset's previous revision — see the decisions log. Those rows are no longer in this tree, so this board cites the finding instead of drawing it; ollama never made the mistake in the first place, its runner moves MoE tensors to system memory on its own. Power and energy

Measured on three meters: the GPU (NVML ∫P dt), the CPU package (RAPL, M3.6) and the whole box at the wall socket (M3.7). This rung is why the harness grew the second and third — fig 13 shows the story reversing between bases.

Blocked on data, not on design

Figures this board would carry if the tree had the rows.

  • needs a runn_cpu_moe searched per objective

    The v1 funnel measures one placement and pins it everywhere, which is precisely what costs us the deep end (fig 05). A max-context probe allowed to move the pin as it climbs would free VRAM for KV exactly when it is needed — the harness's answer to ollama's dynamic placement. Deferred, and the finding as published does not depend on it.

  • needs a runthe power knee

    No power probe was run on this tree. It is the most interesting candidate in the series: decode here is part CPU-bound, so the card's 150 W driver floor may cost even less than the 0.3 % it cost the 0.8B.

  • needs hardwarethe second card

    32 GB of VRAM would hold this model's weights outright, turning the entire placement question off and making this the enthusiast tier's headline comparison.

  • needs a runbatched throughput

    Single-stream only. A MoE is the most interesting model in the series for concurrency — the experts that fire differ per request — and the natural vLLM subject.
